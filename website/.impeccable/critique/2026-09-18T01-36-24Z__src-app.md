## P1 — cross-route anchors landed at the outgoing page's maximum scroll

Measured at 1512×900, sampled every 250ms for 6–8s after each click. The correct resting position for `#benchmarks` is scrollY 3665, heading at 92px.

| case | landed | heading at | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct load `/#benchmarks` | 3573 | 183.5px | −92px |
| same-page click on `/` | 3573 | 183.5px | −92px |
| from `/docs/primeiros-passos/instalacao/` | 1659 | 2097.5px | −2006px |
| from `/docs/referencia/benchmarks/` | 659 | 3097.5px | −3006px |
| from instalacao under `reduce` | 3665 | 91.5px | 0 |

Two distinct causes, both deterministic.

**The 92px error is a double subtraction.** `html` carried `scroll-padding-top: 92px` and every anchor target also carried `scroll-margin-top: 92px` — and the browser's scroll-into-view and Lenis alike subtract *both* (`lenis.mjs:783`). Docs headings carried a third value for the same job, `6.25rem`.

**The 2,000–3,000px error is a stale clamp.** Every landing position equalled the outgoing document's own maximum scroll, exactly: 2559−900=1659 from instalacao, 1559−900=659 from referencia/benchmarks, 3769−844=2925 at 390px. Lenis clamps every scroll to `dimensions.limit` (`lenis.mjs:798`), which at that moment still described the page being left. The seek measured once, on the first frame where the element existed, and never corrected. Under `reduce`, where Lenis never initialises, the same journey was already correct — which is what confined the fault to the smooth-scroll path.

Both are regressions from this line of work, not pre-existing.
